# Open-endedness audit

This audit lists every closed-world assumption found in Crucible (branch `feat/crucible`, commit 8b81276), where it
lives in the code, whether it is *fundamental* (it bounds what can ever happen) or *incidental* (an artefact of how
something was built), what was done about it on `feat/crucible-open-world`, how that is tested, and what remains.
Nothing is removed from this list because it was inconvenient; items that are still closed say so.

Vocabulary. *Mechanism*: finite, developer-written world physics (how money moves, how a machine wears, how a
household chooses). *Label*: a descriptive string a mechanism attaches to an observation (e.g. the loss kind
`capacity_overload`). *Template*: a developer-written description of a solution or of a demand. Open-endedness here
means: the space of things agents can create, and that independent actors can find useful, is not enumerated by
mechanisms, labels or templates - it is generated by composing mechanisms.

## 2. How new demand arises without a catalogue

An organisation's mind measures its own operating journal (`ops`: ledger flows by kind, losses by mechanism). From
it, it derives goals (survive, reduce the largest loss, reduce a cost category that takes a large share of
revenue, grow). It publishes the largest pain as a request with verified evidence and the resources it would
delegate. It screens candidate capabilities - answers to its requests, search on its goals' words, contacts'
recommendations - by an expected value from verified evidence, price, delegation risk and an exploration prior,
and trials the best as a randomised switchback. It adopts if the world-computed effect beats the cost by a margin
scaled to its risk tolerance. Nothing in this chain names a product, a solution or a category: in the two
demonstrated cases the same engine adopted an hourly autoscaler (cloud mechanics) and a telemetry-driven
maintenance service (machine mechanics) that did not exist in the world before an agent wrote them.

Secondary demand is a consequence of mechanisms, not of templates: adopting someone else's service creates a
dependency whose outages become measured `dependency_failure` losses; more automation shifts compute demand,
which moves utilisation-priced compute and hardware investment; better processes free grid capacity; higher
volumes load freight links.

## 3. Answers to the audit questions

* **Which categories are hardcoded?** The 8 legacy need formats (consumer API demand only), the 5 production
  operations, 4 form types, 4 property and 4 attribute dimensions, the loss mechanism labels, ledger kinds, and the
  clause types of contracts. These are mechanisms and labels, not product categories.
* **Which categories are merely labels?** Capability tags and titles, request tags, loss kinds, emergent category
  names (`caps.categories` labels are the most frequent descriptor words).
* **Which outcomes require explicit developer support?** Any value must ultimately reach an actor through a
  mechanism: money (ledger), service availability and quality (cloud, need verifiers), goods attributes (household
  preferences), machine uptime and yields (industry), energy and freight capacity. A capability that improves none of
  these cannot be valued by simulated actors - only by agents (e.g. another learner that pays for it).
* **Can an agent invent a capability outside the original eight needs?** Yes - demonstrated twice (autoscaling,
  predictive maintenance) plus time-of-day pricing and process knowledge; tested in `test_open_substrate.py`,
  `test_open_industry.py`.
* **Can an independent actor adopt it?** Yes, by verified trial; related parties never count (tested).
* **Can a new market form during a world lifetime?** Yes: `caps_markets` records a category once >= 2 independent
  buyers transact; entrants then join it (tested in `test_open_dynamics.py`).
* **Can the invention create secondary demand?** Mechanistically yes (dependency failures, compute utilisation,
  grid and freight load); in the recorded runs the observed secondary effects were competitive entry and upstream
  investment; no new problem category was reported in the 40-45 day runs (`docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`).
* **Can technologies be composed in unforeseen ways?** Services may call any services (observed call graph),
  flows compose any tools, designs compose any items, capabilities can supersede/improve others. The deepest
  observed composition chain in the recorded runs is short (see the census in `open_civilization.json`).
* **Can a new organisation structure have real effects?** Yes: constitutions change who can act and how fast
  (votes take simulated time; owners can become bottlenecks; delegation is capped), and profit-sharing rules
  change members' effort through their incentive (`gov.effort`) - tested in `test_open_institutions.py`.
* **Can social relationships affect later decisions?** Yes: trust from kept/broken agreements and public
  reputation change acceptance of later offers (demonstration 3; tested).
* **Can the environment support new forms of cooperation / institutions?** Contracts compose clauses; constitutions
  compose rules; agents can create organisations with any of these. Novel institution *types* beyond these
  primitives are not possible.
* **Can inventions propagate through society?** Yes: evidence sharing by mail and forum, imitation by forking open
  source, purchase of process knowledge, licensing of designs.
* **Can agents operate useful software created by others?** Yes: install, call, compose, fork (if open/licensed).
* **Can the system distinguish useful novelty from random novelty?** Partly. Mode B trials distinguish software
  that improves an adopter's books from software that does not (a deliberately poor or irrelevant capability is
  rejected), but daily operating results are noisy (lumpy business purchases), so modest real gains are often not
  significant over 10-16 trial days, and interventions whose effects lag (maintenance shifts breakdown risk across
  days) are measured with carry-over between on and off days. An earlier version of demonstration 4 suggested
  predictive maintenance hurt the chain; that result was caused by a delegation bug (installing a maintenance
  capability made firms stop procurement on "on" days, which also *inflated* their trial outcomes) and is
  withdrawn; with the fix, maintenance improves the chain in the base branch (see `docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`).
* **Are apparent effects grounded in authoritative state?** Yes: every effect reported in the demonstrations is
  computed from the event-sourced state of factual and counterfactual forks.
* **Does the world support genuinely unfamiliar agent strategies?** The action space is the full tool set (≈300
  endpoints) plus arbitrary code; nothing restricts strategies to the ones the controls use.
* **Which behaviours are impossible under the current mechanics?** New physical laws; new attribute dimensions;
  new contract clause types or governance rule types; actors valuing anything not mediated by money, service
  outcomes, goods attributes or production outcomes; persuasion by unverifiable rhetoric (Tier-2 minds ignore free
  text); LLM-mediated social reasoning by NPCs (not built here).
* **Which capabilities remain restricted to templates?** The scripted inventor agents are templates (controls).
  NPC entrepreneurs only imitate (fork open source); they do not invent. Simulated developers implement only the
  legacy need formats.

## 4. Failure modes of fake open-endedness (spec Part 27) - how each is addressed

| Failure mode | Addressed by |
|---|---|
| A. more needs = open-ended | No needs were added; demand is outcome-based. |
| B. names without functionality | Capabilities are live code; adoption requires measured improvement. |
| C. an LLM decides success | No LLM judges anything; trial outcomes are computed from ledgers. |
| D. fixed consumer categories | Organisation demand is outcome-based; households value continuous attributes. |
| E. websites without economic effect | Deployed services act on resources and are paid; effects appear in journals. |
| F. organisations without obligations | Constitutions bind actions; contracts bind payments and deliverables. |
| G. text-only negotiation | Only accepted structured terms become contracts; text is not binding. |
| H. invention without artefacts | Inventions are repositories, deployments, designs, process notes. |
| I. research without experiments | Process knowledge is only publishable with the publisher's own completed runs, verified. |
| J. decorative NPCs | Every mind acts through tools with costs; ablations measure their effect. |
| K. reskinned world types | Families differ in mechanisms; held-out mechanisms exist only in test. |
| L. predefined strategies as emergence | Controls are labelled controls; NPC behaviour is labelled rule-based. |
| M. more events, same possibilities | The census measures structure (adoptions, markets, graph), not event counts. |
| N. universal utility evaluator | None exists; verification modes are separate and limited, documented. |
| O. executing software = valuable | Executing is not enough; it must improve an independent adopter's books. |
| P. fake customers | Related-party exclusion (shared membership, operated services) in adoption and payer counts. |
| Q. no control worlds | Ablations (`open_ablations.json`) and counterfactual forks for every demonstration. |

## 5. Remaining limitations (not solved here)

1. **Tier-3 LLM minds are not wired into the open-world society.** Minds are Tier-2 (rule-based, utility-driven).
   The existing frozen-LLM agent tooling (`agents/llm.py`) can act as an *agent*; NPC language understanding was
   not built. Free text is ignored by NPC decisions.
2. **Switchback trials are noisy and carry over lagged effects** (maintenance, inventory policies): cautious firms
   reject small real gains, risk-tolerant ones sometimes adopt noise; no correction for carry-over is implemented.
3. **Industrial calibration**: with default (unoptimised) operating points, downstream assemblers run operating
   deficits in several seeds while upstream firms profit, and the firm sector's total operating result in the 90-day
   mixed run is negative. The economy functions (production, trade, sales continue for 90 days) but is not tuned
   to a sustainable steady state.
4. **No runtime developer-module extension interface or schema migration** was implemented (row 17).
5. **NPC entrepreneurs imitate, they do not invent**; NPC developers only implement legacy need formats.
6. **Fixed mechanism vocabulary**: operations, attribute dimensions, clause types, rule types, loss mechanisms.
7. **No VM-grade isolation on this host** (no /dev/kvm, no gVisor/bubblewrap; user namespaces refused). The
   backend layer refuses unavailable backends; it does not provide them.
8. Knowledge truth/outdatedness is only modelled for verifiable evidence (trial outcomes, breaches); there is no
   general model of incorrect-but-believed claims beyond unverifiable text being ignored.

## 6. Crucible 3.0 addendum: the 2.0 weaknesses (Part 19), status after this branch

| | weakness | what was done | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | no lasting adoption in the 90-day run | Diagnosed as mostly a *genuine economic outcome* plus short or noisy trials (see below). Adoption persisted in the second-order runs (autoscaler at 1-2 firms, placement at 1-2 firms in seeds 2-3) and in the 120-day run. Finding from the 120-day runs (Part 24): firms whose service instance was terminated for non-payment kept their
listings and a dead service for months. Every day they recorded `service_unavailable` losses, and requests served
fell by about 40% after week 9. Firm minds now restart a terminated service when they can pay for a week of
compute, and wind the company up otherwise (`minds._recover_or_exit`, ablatable with `ablate_recovery`).

Winding firms up then exposed a latent bug. When the offerings with the highest consumer slots closed, the hourly
demand process crashed every hour. Consumers' provider and awareness arrays still referenced the closed slots.
No firm was served for the rest of the run: about 460 requests per week instead of about 300,000. The liveness
checks did not flag it at first, because "demand served > 0" was still true. Fixed in `economy/consumers.py`,
with a regression test in `tests/test_firm_recovery.py`. Lesson: a liveness check needs thresholds relative to
recent levels, not only "> 0". `long_run.py` also records `process_failures` every week, and they were present
here.
