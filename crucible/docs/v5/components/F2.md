# F2 - Institutions, commitments and the labour market

Workstream F2 of Crucible 5.0 (package `crucible.commerce`, files `institutions.py`, `contracts.py`, `labor.py`,
plus the world builders in `orgworld.py`). Requirement owned: **SP-6.6** (MASTER_SPEC Part 6.6, adaptable
institutions within stable constraints). The labour market also supplies the mechanics of Part 9.9 (labour
marketplace) and the organisational parts of Part 9.12 for the Tier 2 domains (workstream I).

Everything is built on the 4.0 services rather than beside them: organisations are identity orgs, membership is
identity membership (invitations are accepted, ownership is accepted), delegation is identity grants, employment is
4.0 `contracts` (salary payment + membership + notice), companies wind up through `business`, money moves only
through `bank`, and charter enforcement joins the identity authorisation hook `gov_check` next to the 4.0 `gov`
constitution check.

## Kernel modules

All three are registered from `crucible/commerce/modules.py` (imported by `crucible.load_all_modules()`) and pass
`crucible.worldgen.admission.admit` (schemas, no crashes, invariants, replay, regeneration, snapshot/fork,
visibility, protected hooks).

### `inst` (host `inst`) - institutions

| what | tools |
|---|---|
| charter (first adoption; afterwards only by an `amend` decision) | `inst.adopt`, `inst.charter` |
| decisions | `inst.propose {org, action}`, `inst.vote`, `inst.delegate_vote`, `inst.decisions` |
| admission | `inst.join` (open admission, or an application decided by the charter's `admit` procedure) |
| shared projects / ventures | `inst.project_create`, `inst.project_join`, `inst.project_fund`, `inst.task_add`, `inst.allocate`, `inst.task_assign`, `inst.task_submit`, `inst.task_review`, `inst.project`, `inst.project_close` |

A **charter** has exactly these fields - `procedures`, `rules`, `default`, `membership`, `offices`,
`dissolution` - and nothing else:

```json
{"procedures": {"board": {"type": "supermajority", "electorate": "admins", "window_s": 259200},
                "labour": {"type": "weighted", "weights": "contribution"},
                "treasurer": {"type": "officer", "office": "treasurer"}},
 "rules": [{"match": {"kind": "tool", "tool": "*", "min_amount": 1000000}, "procedure": "labour"},
           {"match": {"kind": "tool", "tool": "bank.transfer"}, "procedure": "treasurer"},
           {"match": {"kind": "amend"}, "procedure": "board"}],
 "default": "board",
 "membership": {"admission": "decision", "max_members": 60},
 "offices": {"treasurer": {"holder": "p:ada", "budget_cents_month": 300000, "tools": ["bank.transfer"]}},
 "dissolution": {"distribution": "shares"}}
```

Procedure types: `majority`, `supermajority`, `unanimous`, `weighted` (weights `shares` | `contribution` |
`tenure`), `delegated` (liquid democracy: weight flows along `inst.delegate_vote` chains, a direct vote overrides,
cycles abstain), `consensus` (passes at the window's end unless someone objects), `officer` (an office holder
decides alone within the office's monthly budget). Electorates: `members`, `admins`, `owners`, `shareholders`,
`unit:<id>`, and `partners` for ventures. The electorate and its weights are frozen when a decision is proposed.
A proposer who is an elector votes for its own proposal. Rules match an action kind, a tool pattern, a minimum
amount and optionally a condition `when` - a metrics predicate about the organisation itself. The first matching
rule decides; otherwise `default`; otherwise the owners unanimously.

**Enforcement**: a tool run in the organisation's name that a rule governs is refused - for owners too, and also
when it runs under an approved 4.0 `gov` proposal - unless it is the execution of an approved `inst` decision, or
an officer acts within budget. Where a 4.0 `gov` constitution also governs the action (non-tool actions are
checked by it as `inst.<kind>`), both must be satisfied.

Decision actions (`action.kind`): `tool` (any non-operator, non-read-only tool, executed as the organisation under
ordinary authorisation), `amend`, `admit`, `expel`, `role` (member/admin only; ownership is never assigned),
`unit_create` (a unit's head holds an office `unit:<id>` with the unit's budget), `unit_assign`, `unit_dissolve`,
`office`, `office_remove`, `allocate` (organisation account -> project pool, or pool -> task reward),
`project_close`, `merge` + `absorb` (both organisations decide; members are invited, money and projects pass, the
absorbed organisation's contracts end; refused while it owes anything), `spin_off` (proposed by the unit's head, who
founds the new organisation; optionally a registered company funded by the parent, which keeps up to 49%),
`dissolve` (projects close and refund, units and offices end; a company winds up through the registry - creditors
first, then shareholders, refused if insolvent; any other organisation pays its claims and distributes by the
charter's basis). Ventures (decisions with `org` = a project id) decide `allocate`, `project_close`,
`admit_partner`.

**Projects** hold a pool in `acct_inst_escrow` funded by recorded contributions; tasks have required skills, effort
hours and dependencies on existing tasks (so no cycles); rewards are allocated from the pool and paid on completion
(a simulated worker's measured completion, or the lead's acceptance of an agent's submission). Closing a project
returns what is left to the money contributors pro rata. Leads may delegate with `id.grant {cap: "inst.lead",
scope: <project>}`.

**Stable constraints** - refused whatever the organisation decides: charter keys outside the six fields (with the
law named: core accounting law, security boundary, hidden evaluation criteria, world state); conditions on metrics
without a declared subject (evaluator-only) or about anyone but the organisation; operator (`admin`) tools; action
kinds outside the closed set; assigning ownership; spending other principals' money or granting authority over
their resources (refused by ordinary authorisation when the decision executes); any money movement except `bank`'s.

### `commit` (host `commit`) - escrowed commitments, negotiation, disputes

`commit.offer` / `commit.respond {accept|reject|counter}` / `commit.withdraw` / `commit.inbox` / `commit.thread`
negotiate terms (`$me`/`$you`, a payer, 1-8 milestones, optional independent arbiter with a fee of at most 10% of
the smallest milestone, review window, dispute window, default split). Acceptance moves the payer's money into
`acct_commit_escrow` (full authority over the account is required: a limited spending grant cannot commit money).
Deliverables: `acceptance` (deliver -> accept, dispute, or silence releases after the review window), `predicate`
(checked by the world at the due time; scoped to the parties' own data), `task` (an `inst` task done by the payee
at a minimum measured quality). A missed due date refunds the payer and is a public breach. Disputes resolve by
`commit.settle` (both post the same split), `commit.rule` (the arbiter, paid its fee) or the default split when the
dispute window closes. `commit.record` is a public track record (counts only). On winding up, delivered work is
paid, disputes take the default split and undelivered milestones are refunded.

### `labor` (host `labor`) - labour market

Simulated workers (config `{"workers": n}`, or `Labor.create_sim_worker` / `add_worker` for world generators) are
persons of kind `npc` with private skills over eight skills (`software, data, design, writing, research,
operations, sales, management`), interests, hours, wage weight, patience and a reservation wage. Agents register with
`labor.register` and do assigned work themselves. Tools: `labor.post`, `labor.close_post`, `labor.postings`,
`labor.apply`, `labor.applicants`, `labor.profile`, `labor.offer`, `labor.respond {accept|reject|counter}`,
`labor.offers`, `labor.adjust_pay`, `labor.dismiss`, `labor.team`.

Worker agency (each step is a tool call by the worker's own principal, from its own state and public data):
job search and applications; accept / counter / reject an offer against its ask (reservation, or 115% of its current
pay, times the employer's public risk - payment breaches, commitment breaches, company age - adjusted by interest
in the work); decline assignments it has no hours for or from organisations it does not work for; daily work on
unblocked tasks with productivity from skill match and effort (satisfaction and fatigue); quality measured from skill
match plus seeded per-task noise; satisfaction from pay versus the best posting it qualifies for, interest, fatigue
and unpaid wages; quitting with the contract's notice when unhappy and a better offer exists (or very unhappy);
leaving and blacklisting an employer that breaches its contract; voting in its organisation's decisions from its own
interest (`on_inst_decision`). Hours worked are recorded as labour contributions valued at the wage
(`inst_contributions`, and the `labor_hours:<org>` weights that 4.0 `gov` profit sharing by labour reads).

Evaluator metrics (goals/metrics.py, subject = the organisation, usable in contract deliverables between parties):
`labor_employees`, `labor_retention`, `project_completion`, `project_quality`, `project_delivered`.

## Using it

```python
import crucible; crucible.load_all_modules()
from crucible.commerce import orgworld as OW
w = OW.create(seed=1, workers=16)              # identity, bank, admin, contracts, business, gov, deal, inst, commit, labor
ada, _ = OW.join(w, "ada", 50_000_000)
org, acct = OW.found_company(w, ada, "acme", 20_000_000)
OW.act(w, ada, "inst.adopt", org=org, charter={...})
OW.act(w, ada, "labor.post", org=org, title="engineer", skills={"software": 0.4}, salary_max=900_000)
w.advance_time(w.now + 3 * 86400)               # simulated workers apply
```

`orgworld.spec(seed, workers=..., extra=(...))` returns the `WorldSpec` for a domain's own world family;
`orgworld.verify_reproducible(w)` replays and regenerates a world built from genesis.

## Limitations

* Simulated workers and their daily work are per-person dictionary records with about three events per worker-day;
  this is fine for thousands of workers, not for population scale. Backing workers by the columnar population
  (`pop.skills`, `pop.employer`, `ids.ensure_principal`) belongs with the substrate (workstream A), which was not
  merged when this was written.
* Cross-module notifications use chained 4.0 hooks (`gov_check`, `on_member_left`, `on_contract_ended`,
  `on_task_assigned`, `on_inst_decision`), not `substrate.bus` subscriptions.
* A charter governs tools by name pattern and amount (`"tool": "*"` with `min_amount` covers every spending tool);
  institutional (non-tool) actions are governed by a 4.0 `gov` constitution only through rules matching `inst.<kind>`.
* On absorption, the absorbed organisation's employment contracts end (its people are invited to the acquirer but
  must be re-hired); merger consideration for the absorbed company's shareholders is not modelled.
* An agent's own work is verified by the lead's acceptance (no measured quality) unless the task is the deliverable
  of a commitment with a predicate; only simulated work has a measured quality in the verified record.
* The simulated vote model is simple (self-interest, distrust of self-payment, satisfaction-driven approval); it is
  not calibrated against data.
